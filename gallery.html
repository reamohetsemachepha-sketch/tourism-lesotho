DESTINATIONS

Maletsunyane Waterfall.

Maletsunyane Falls is one of the highest single-drop waterfalls in Africa, plunging 192 meters (630 feet) over a dramatic ledge of Triassic-Jurassic basalt rock. Located near the highland town of Semonkong in Lesotho’s "Kingdom in the Sky," this powerful waterfall cascades into a spectacular canyon, creating a thunderous roar and a perpetual plume of mist.

Mohale dam.

Mohale Dam is the highest concrete-faced rock-fill dam in Africa, standing at a towering 145 meters (476 feet) high and stretching 700 meters across the Senqunyane River valley. Built at a cost of roughly $1.5 billion as Phase 1B of the massive Lesotho Highlands Water Project (LHWP), it acts as a backup reservoir to the iconic Katse Dam, with which it is connected via a massive 32-kilometer subterranean gravity tunnel.

Katse Dam.

Katse Dam is the crown jewel of Lesotho's engineering feats and the second-largest double-curvature arch dam in Africa. Towering at a breathtaking 185 meters (607 feet) high with a crest length of 710 meters, it serves as the centerpiece of Phase 1A of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project (LHWP). Built across the deep Malibamatso River valley, this monumental concrete structure traps a massive alpine reservoir that supplies water to South Africa's Gauteng province and generates hydro-electricity for Lesotho.

Traditional huts.

The Basotho traditional hut, known as a Mokhoro, is a masterpiece of indigenous eco-design perfectly adapted to the harsh alpine climate of Lesotho. These round, windowless stone structures dotting the Maloti Mountains are built entirely from local, natural materials. They are meticulously engineered to keep families warm during freezing highland winters and cool under the scorching summer sun.

Highlands Landscapes.

The Maluti Mountains (also spelled Maloti) form the rugged, alpine spine of Lesotho and are part of the greater Drakensberg-Maloti system. This immense, snow-capped range covers over 70% of the country, earning Lesotho its moniker as the "Kingdom in the Sky." With a basalt layer over 1,400 meters thick formed by ancient volcanic eruptions, the Malutis are home to the highest average elevation of any mountain range in Africa, with numerous peaks soaring past 3,000 meters (10,000 feet).

Mount Qiloane.

Mount Qiloane is a legendary, cone-shaped sandstone mountain that stands as the ultimate visual symbol of Lesotho's national identity. Rising 1,719 meters (5,640 feet) above sea level just east of Maseru, its striking silhouette is famously crowned by a sharp, 30-meter pillar of cave sandstone that tapers incrementally to a point.Mount Qiloane's most profound claim to fame is its shape: it is the direct inspiration behind the design of the (Mokorotlo hat).

Mokorotlong Maseru.

The Mokorotlo (plural: Mokorotlong) is the iconic, conical woven straw hat that serves as the ultimate national symbol of Lesotho. Deeply intertwined with the heritage of the Basotho people, its distinct architectural silhouette is famously inspired by the nearby conical peak of Mount Qiloane. It is so central to the country's identity that it sits directly at the heart of the Lesotho National Flag, on vehicle license plates, and on local currency.

Our Lady of Victories Archcarthedral.

Our Lady of Victories Cathedral (also known as the Maseru Cathedral) is a prominent Roman Catholic landmark located in the heart of Maseru, Lesotho. Constructed out of locally sourced sandstone, it blends traditional context with post-war architectural design and stands as the spiritual headquarters for the Metropolitan Archdiocese of Maseru.

Ts'ehlanyane National Park.

Ts’ehlanyane National Park is Lesotho's premier national park, protecting a breathtaking 56-square-kilometer patch of rugged mountain wilderness in the Maloti Mountains. Located in the Leribe District, this high-altitude haven sits between 2,000m and 3,000m above sea level, preserving one of the country's only remaining stands of rare indigenous forest and serving as a critical part of the Maloti-Drakensberg Transfrontier Conservation Area.

Highlands Landscapes.

Connecting the Maluti Mountains to Lesobeng takes you deep into the true heart of Lesotho’s isolated central highlands. Located in the Thaba-Tseka District, Lesobeng is a remote community hidden within a dramatic landscape of deeply incised valleys, rugged sandstone cliffs, and towering basalt formations. Unlike tourist hubs like Semonkong, Lesobeng remains largely untouched by mass tourism, offering raw, unfiltered access to highland mountain life.

Thaba-Bosiu Cultural Village.

The Thaba Bosiu Cultural Village is one of Lesotho's premier heritage destinations, located 24 km northeast of Maseru at the foot of the historic Thaba Bosiu plateau. It serves as a living museum dedicated to preserving and showcasing the rich history, architecture, and traditions of the Basotho nation.

Afriski

Afriski Mountain Resort is the premier ski and all-year adventure destination in Lesotho, famously recognized as one of the few ski resorts on the African continent. Perched high in the Maluti Mountains at an elevation of 3,050 meters (reaching up to 3,222 meters), it offers a highly unique high-altitude alpine experience.
