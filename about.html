About Lesotho

Culture and Identity

  • The People: An individual is called a Mosotho, and the people as a collective are known as the Basotho.
  • National Symbols: The mokorotlo (a traditional conical straw hat) and the iconic woolen Basotho blanket worn by both men and women as a symbol of status and heritage.
  • History: Founded as a unified nation in the 19th century by King Moshoeshoe I, who used the rugged terrain to protect his people from surrounding conflicts.

Key Facts

  • Capital & Largest City: Maseru
  • Population: ~2.1 to 2.3 million people
  • Area: 30,355 square kilometers (11,720 square miles)
  • Official Languages: Sesotho and English
  • Currency: Lesotho Loti (plural: Maloti), which is pegged 1:1 with the South African Rand
  • Government: Unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy (Current King: Letsie III)
  • Independence Day: October 4, 1966 (from Great Britain)

Economy

  • Key Sectors: Subsistence agriculture, livestock, diamond mining (such as the Letšeng mine), and textile/apparel manufacturing.
  • Water & Energy: The massive Lesotho Highlands Water Project dams rivers in the Maloti mountains to export fresh water and hydroelectric power to South Africa, serving as a vital revenue stream for the country.

Geography & Climate

  • The "Kingdom in the Sky": It is the only independent country on Earth that lies entirely above 1,000 meters in elevation. Its lowest point is 1,400 meters, and its highest peak is Thabana Ntlenyana at 3,482 meters (11,424 feet).
  • Climate: Temperate with cool-to-cold, dry winters (frequent snow in the highlands) and hot, wet summers. It also features Afriski, a prominent ski resort.
  • Terrain: Dominated by the Maloti and Drakensberg mountain ranges.
